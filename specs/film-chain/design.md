# Film chain design

- Scanner vocabulary follows [Scanner language](spec.md#scanner-language). The canonical conversion mode is `negative_to_positive`; scan output validation owns its accepted values. CLI, presets, recipes, GUI state, and diagnostics consume that vocabulary without aliases. The UI separates display labels from serialized values.
- The naming change retains the existing runtime fields, document schema versions, conversion methods, defaults, calibration, processing order, and numerical operations. Telemetry uses the conversion name for its stage and execution reasons. Saved look controls and acquisition/finish provenance keep their existing ownership.
- `core::runtime` is the production entry point. `Pipeline` owns stage topology and state.
- Reference conversion follows the [Digital Reference design](digital-reference/design.md). Its matched receiver and common response belong to Scan preparation. The reconstruction invariants below describe the existing methods.
- Pipeline entry points share optical diagnostic tap admission and selector-conflict rules. Each entry point retains its validation order and topology-specific guards.
- Stage preparation derives film curves, spectral data, scan settings, color transforms, and working pixel pitch once for the selected execution.
- `ComputeBackend` separates stage orchestration from CPU and WGPU execution. Both must implement the [target optical order](spec.md#scan-optics-and-print-viewing-glare) and [GPU numerical alignment](../gpu-execution/design.md#numerical-alignment). WGPU owns validated device representations, resources, and dispatch.
- Scan preparation resolves one `ScanReceiver` before positive calibration. The receiver owns spectral sensitivity, reference levels, source, calibration status, and versioned provenance. `legacy_colorimetric` preserves the existing spectral-to-output contract; approximate and measured receivers are separate profiles.
- `lab_scan_approx` uses the pinned Fotufilm research snapshot `20261012`, `Sources/FotufilmCore/PrintPaperTables.swift:296-305`: Gaussian LED bands centered at 630, 545, and 465 nm with sigma 8.5, 14, and 9.5 nm, sampled on the production 380–780 nm / 5 nm grid. Illuminated bands are normalized to clear capture `[1,1,1]`. Detector RGB is assigned to linear sRGB and transformed to the requested output space. Both the bands and this display assignment are approximate and uncalibrated; version `fotufilm_lab_scan_approx_v1` is available through serializable receiver provenance. The legacy path retains its CIE capture and historical transform.
- Receiver acquisition serves `direct_scan` and the capture-based positive methods for the same render; `density` reads record densities and acquires no capture. Capture sharing and convert-film handling follow the [product contract](spec.md#scanner-receiver-and-interpretation).
- Bulk scanning and scalar forward calibration share log-XYZ LUT construction and density-coordinate scaling. Each path retains its own sample precision, interpolation, correction, and color-transform order.
- Exposure reconstruction has three preparation-time methods. `density` reads record densities, as a narrowband scanner does, and derives one private per-record response from the selected profile density curves: monotone density-to-log-exposure knots with finite endpoint slopes. One record's output must not depend on another record's density, and image pixels reuse the response without any spectral capture. `separable` reuses the existing per-channel monotone inverse on a receiver capture, converting the capture to density through its per-channel clear reference. `coupled` samples the actual receiver response over a 33-point-per-axis film dye-density grid and splits each cell into six tetrahedra with shared face triangulations. A fixed signed `asinh` coordinate preserves negative output-RGB samples. Preparation must reject singular, folded, and poorly conditioned cells under the CPU f64 reference criteria and [GPU numerical alignment](../gpu-execution/design.md#numerical-alignment). A bounding-volume tree locates interior tetrahedra; image pixels use affine interpolation and never run an iterative solver. Monochrome uses a constrained neutral one-dimensional inverse.
- The density response uses least-squares isotonic regression on the profile density samples to regularize reversals. Pooled plateaus use their mean log exposure. The forward film curves remain unchanged.
- Coupled exterior capture uses the nearest physical boundary triangle and a shared vertex derivative field. Raw-capture tangent continuation is chained through the local monotone density-to-log-exposure slope and positive tone derivative. Nonfinite or unrepresentable queries and outputs fail before publication. The forward film curves remain unchanged.
- Every existing exposure-reconstruction method reads the profile density curves in the domain the film stage emits: each record measured over its own minimum. Calibration rows, record axes, and the lab-scan density references use that domain, so a record's curve minimum is never read as exposure.
- Scan setup is a prepared immutable value. Its measurement, derivation, invalidation, and reuse follow the [product contract](spec.md#scanner-receiver-and-interpretation).
- Per-frame setup belongs to scanner output interpretation. It must not mutate film exposure, development, film-domain effects, camera exposure, or the convert-film solve. Real negative captures must not receive virtual camera metering.
- Scanner finishing is a separate output stage after positive interpretation. It owns neutral or LabScan-style appearance and strength; it must not alter receiver acquisition, positive calibration, route topology, or direct negative polarity.
- Receiver, interpretation, setup, and finish decisions are part of the prepared scan identity and cache key. Their provenance and version must remain available to diagnostics and serialized render state.
- For the capture-based methods, stage preparation derives one private calibrated scanner response from the selected profile density curves and the actual scan color context, retaining existing development dependencies. The response stores per-channel monotone capture-to-log-exposure knots and finite endpoint slopes; image pixels reuse it without rebuilding spectral calibration. No separate creative-response reference preparation is introduced.
- Scanner calibration uses least-squares isotonic regression on the scanned neutral density samples to regularize measurement reversals. Pooled plateaus use their mean log exposure. The forward film curves remain unchanged.
- Positive interpretation is shared by direct `input > film > scan` and real negative-capture `input > convert-film > scan`. The convert-film scan passes its selected scan illuminant into the same preparation path. Camera exposure compensation is applied only to the simulated direct-film route; the real scanner capture is already in scanner exposure space.
- The scanned medium determines the stage topology and the scanner calibration references.
- Scanner white and black correction derives its references from the scanned medium: film zero and maximum dye density for a slide scan, and the print exposure of the film density range for a photographic-paper scan.
- Exposure reconstruction and the film-scan black and white points must not estimate their response or their points from image content.
- Existing interpretation controls parameterize the reconstruction response only. They must not modify the film curves or the printing exposure.
- The black and white points reuse references that the chain already derives: the relative log-exposure axis of the derived inverse for a negative scan, and the zero and maximum dye density references of scanner white and black correction for a slide scan. They must not introduce a second density model.
- The black and white points and scanner white and black correction are separate controls. The points must not modify the film exposure, and adding them must not change the correction.
- CPU and GPU execute one photographic stage contract under [GPU execution](../gpu-execution/design.md#ownership). A resident chain is an execution strategy, not a second model or permission for CPU image-stage fallback.
- Runtime owns complete-operation capability validation, preparation, stage order, and output finalization. Known GPU limitations must be checked before dispatch; an explicit GPU request must fail rather than use a hidden CPU stage.
- The WGPU backend owns device-resident stage execution and resource lifetimes. Multiple submissions or bounded tiles must preserve the same domains and do not require a second public pipeline.
- Filming metering owns the downsample, decode, luminance measurement, and exposure calculation. The pipeline meters the full input before crop and resize.
- Private Jzazbz and CAM16 modules own their appearance-model formulas and viewing adaptation. Output compression retains destination policy and the shared table cache.
- The WGPU blur module owns standalone Gaussian blur execution and shared blur limits remain backend policy. Resident stage-specific blur dispatch remains with its owning stage.
- Scanner preparation must place the optical kernel at the linear readout boundary before any inverse or point map. Constant linear receiver matrices may commute with the shared achromatic blur only with equivalent boundary handling and measured precision. Nonlinear transforms may not commute with blur. A destination-encoded or gamut-processed buffer is not an optical input.
- Print viewing glare must be added after optical blur in that linear readout boundary. A fixed linear color transform may carry its illuminant-colored offset into another linear coordinate system; nonlinear correction must remain downstream. Its `print_render` storage and preset ownership do not make it print development.
- Resident dispatch must include optical order in capability decisions and prepared identity. Diagnostic final-RGB paths must share the order; material-density taps must remain before scanner optics. The historical late-blur shortcut must be removed, not preserved under identity points or a backend-specific branch.
- Working pixel pitch is carried with the image through crop and resize and is consumed by spatial stages; stages do not infer it from the current raster size.
- Color-domain transitions are explicit. The chain does not apply an implicit transfer curve or primaries conversion inside an effect.
- Stage preparation resolves the capture illuminant once per render from the selected film profile's `reference_illuminant` and the explicit `camera.capture_illuminant` leaf, under the [capture light boundary](spec.md#capture-light-and-scan-light). It reuses the shared illuminant selection and validator that the scan and enlarger illuminants already use, so one service owns every light-source name. A resolved capture illuminant participates in the prepared render identity and its cache key; an unset leaf does not.
- No generic graph or backend-specific public parameter schema is required; runtime parameters remain the single caller-facing model.
- [Output mix and material controls](../output-mix/design.md) owns preflash exposure composition, Output lightness retirement, and final mixing. Per-stage, resident, converted-film, and diagnostic paths execute only material operations present in their route. Existing chemistry and scanner reference ownership remain unchanged.
- The shared output finishing boundary delegates CMYK ink style to [CMYK ink style](../cmyk-style/design.md). It must also serve the film-free `input` workflow without preparing a spectral chain.
