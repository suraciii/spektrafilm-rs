# Film chain design

- Scanner vocabulary follows [Scanner language](spec.md#scanner-language). The canonical scan output mode is `negative_to_positive`; scan output validation owns its accepted values. CLI, presets, recipes, GUI state, and diagnostics consume that vocabulary without aliases. The UI separates display labels from serialized values.
- The scan model retains the existing runtime field names, document schema versions, defaults, calibration, processing order, and numerical operations for the controls it keeps. Telemetry uses the scan output name for its stage and execution reasons. The scanner lamp participates in the prepared scan identity and its cache key.
- `core::runtime` is the production entry point. `Pipeline` owns stage topology and state.
- Pipeline entry points share optical diagnostic tap admission and selector-conflict rules. Each entry point retains its validation order and topology-specific guards.
- Stage preparation derives film curves, spectral data, scan settings, color transforms, and working pixel pitch once for the selected execution.
- `ComputeBackend` separates stage orchestration from CPU and WGPU execution. Both must implement the [target optical order](spec.md#scan-optics-and-print-viewing-glare) and [GPU numerical alignment](../gpu-execution/design.md#numerical-alignment). WGPU owns validated device representations, resources, and dispatch.
- Scan preparation resolves one colorimetric sensor: the CIE 1931 color-matching functions over the production wavelength grid. No receiver profile is selectable, and no receiver calibration matrix is applied. The scan matrix is the identity.
- The scanner lamp resolves through the shared illuminant service that `enlarger.illuminant` uses, so one service owns every light-source name. The lamp's spectral power distribution is prepared once per render and participates in the prepared scan identity and its cache key.
- Scan acquisition is one spectral integration of the scanned medium under the lamp, taken against the clear film base of that medium as the white reference. The acquisition chromatically adapts from that clear-film white to the output color space's white and emits output-space linear RGB.
- Bulk scanning and scalar forward calibration share log-XYZ LUT construction and density-coordinate scaling. Each path retains its own sample precision, interpolation, correction, and color-transform order.
- Negative conversion has one preparation-time interpretation. It reads record densities, as a narrowband scanner does, and derives one private per-record response from the selected profile density curves: monotone density-to-log-exposure knots with finite endpoint slopes. One record's output must not depend on another record's density, and image pixels reuse the response without any spectral capture.
- The positive tone that the density reading passes through is one logistic curve family. `scanner.conversion_style` selects which declared constants that family uses: `neutral` is the frozen reference constants, and any other name resolves to `data/scan-styles/<name>.json` carrying one `brightness_ev`/`contrast`/`dmax` triple per channel. The style changes the constants only; it adds no curve family and no per-image fitting.
- A conversion style is declared data resolved once at pipeline construction and refreshed by parameter edits that change it; the pipeline carries the resolved per-channel constants, and the resident fences capture that resolved triple. A `direct_scan` does not apply a style, so the style slot is inert there.
- No conversion style ships with the product. `neutral` is the one built-in style; every other style is a user-authored file, so the style catalog is the data directory's `scan-styles/` folder and nothing more.
- The density response uses least-squares isotonic regression on the profile density samples to regularize reversals. Pooled plateaus use their mean log exposure. The forward film curves remain unchanged.
- Every exposure-reconstruction path reads the profile density curves in the domain the film stage emits: each record measured over its own minimum. Calibration rows and record axes use that domain, so a record's curve minimum is never read as exposure.
- Negative interpretation is shared by direct `input > film > scan` and real negative-capture `input > convert-film > scan`. Camera exposure compensation is applied only to the simulated direct-film route; the real scanner capture is already in scanner exposure space.
- The scanned medium determines the stage topology and the scanner calibration references.
- Exposure reconstruction must not estimate its response from image content.
- The scanner owns no automatic setup and no scanner finish. No appearance treatment runs between negative conversion and output gamut processing.
- Scan provenance serializes the neutral scan model identity: the CIE 1931 sensor, the clear-film white reference, the output space white, the identity scan matrix, and the resolved lamp.
- CPU and GPU execute one photographic stage contract under [GPU execution](../gpu-execution/design.md#ownership). A resident chain is an execution strategy, not a second model or permission for CPU image-stage fallback.
- Runtime owns complete-operation capability validation, preparation, stage order, and output finalization. Known GPU limitations must be checked before dispatch; an explicit GPU request must fail rather than use a hidden CPU stage.
- The WGPU backend owns device-resident stage execution and resource lifetimes. Multiple submissions or bounded tiles must preserve the same domains and do not require a second public pipeline.
- Filming metering owns the downsample, decode, luminance measurement, and exposure calculation. The pipeline meters the full input before crop and resize.
- Private Jzazbz and CAM16 modules own their appearance-model formulas and viewing adaptation. Output compression retains destination policy and the shared table cache.
- The WGPU blur module owns standalone Gaussian blur execution and shared blur limits remain backend policy. Resident stage-specific blur dispatch remains with its owning stage.
- Scanner preparation must place the optical kernel at the linear readout boundary before any inverse. Constant linear sensor matrices may commute with the shared achromatic blur only with equivalent boundary handling and measured precision. Nonlinear transforms may not commute with blur. A destination-encoded or gamut-processed buffer is not an optical input.
- Print viewing glare must be added after optical blur in that linear readout boundary. A fixed linear color transform may carry its illuminant-colored offset into another linear coordinate system; nonlinear correction must remain downstream. Its `print_render` storage and preset ownership do not make it print development.
- Resident dispatch must include optical order in capability decisions and prepared identity. Diagnostic final-RGB paths must share the order; material-density taps must remain before scanner optics. The historical late-blur shortcut must be removed, not preserved under identity points or a backend-specific branch.
- Working pixel pitch is carried with the image through crop and resize and is consumed by spatial stages; stages do not infer it from the current raster size.
- Color-domain transitions are explicit. The chain does not apply an implicit transfer curve or primaries conversion inside an effect.
- Stage preparation resolves the capture illuminant once per render from the selected film profile's `reference_illuminant` and the explicit `camera.capture_illuminant` leaf, under the [capture light boundary](spec.md#capture-light-and-scan-light). It reuses the shared illuminant selection and validator that the scanner lamp and enlarger illuminant already use, so one service owns every light-source name. A resolved capture illuminant participates in the prepared render identity and its cache key; an unset leaf does not.
- No generic graph or backend-specific public parameter schema is required; runtime parameters remain the single caller-facing model.
- [Output mix and material controls](../output-mix/design.md) owns preflash exposure composition, Output lightness retirement, and final mixing. Per-stage, resident, converted-film, and diagnostic paths execute only material operations present in their route. Existing chemistry and scanner reference ownership remain unchanged.
- The shared output finishing boundary delegates CMYK ink style to [CMYK ink style](../cmyk-style/design.md). It must also serve the film-free `input` workflow without preparing a spectral chain.
