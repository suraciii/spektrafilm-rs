# LUT delivery

## Contract

- LUT delivery builds a deterministic transform from the selected base workflow, color settings, and runtime controls. Optional [magazine print color](../magazine-print-color/spec.md) is finishing behavior, not a photographic-print calibration claim.
- A LUT bake contains no image-specific spatial effects or stochastic grain. The bundle records any controls neutralized for baking.
- The bundle may contain canonical LUT stages, contiguous collapsed sub-chains, machine-readable metadata, OCIO wiring, and QA results.
- LUT domains, ranges, color spaces, stock selections, and wire constants are part of the bundle contract. A consumer must not cross-chain files from different bundles.
- Target validation rejects unsupported inputs, outputs, topology, resolution, or artifact references before baking.
- The bundle records the bake parameters and provenance needed to reproduce or audit its output.
- Bundles with active magazine color must record the base workflow and finishing definition independently. They must use an explicit `magazine_` final-stage role so they cannot be mistaken for photographic-print LUT stages.
- [Film and print digital response](../material-response/spec.md) must bake at its owning material stage: Film response in actual film development, and Print response in print exposure and development. Both change physical record density before scanning. Film response must not re-develop a real captured negative on conversion routes; Print response must not run without actual print development. Finished RGB runs neither. The bundle must record response controls, effective stage eligibility, and definition version while retaining the physical meanings of film-density and print-density wires.
- Print split-toning masks must use the local unmodified material baseline defined by [Print exposure composition](../material-response/design.md#print-exposure-composition). They must be deterministic and LUT-bakeable, without whole-image statistics, auto-tuning, or final RGB split-toning substitutions. Scan reference preparation must exclude creative responses and must not inverse-recalibrate them away.
- Output mix below 1 must be supported only by a complete one-LUT source-to-output bake with the prepared source branch expressed in the same transport and geometry. Segmented topologies must reject that selection before baking because their scan-stage input does not carry the source branch. The baker must not silently reset output amount to 1. Image-specific geometry remains neutralized under the existing LUT contract.
- When material response and Magazine appearance are active, response must already be included in the upstream developed record, and Magazine appearance must run once at its existing post-scan finishing boundary. The final RGB stage retains its `magazine_` role and records the definitions separately. Neither a segmented consumer nor a collapsed complete transform may reapply response or Magazine appearance. Overall output mix follows Magazine appearance in linear output RGB; a complete one-LUT mixed transform must retain the appearance provenance even though intermediate nodes are collapsed.
- Existing output gamut algorithms remain unchanged except for the retirement of standalone lightness compression under [Output boundary and retirement](../material-response/spec.md#output-boundary-and-retirement). A bake must not substitute final RGB response, an output lightness stage, or a new Finished RGB output-processing switch for material response.
