# Lab report vision

## Purpose

A lab record states what changed. The report shows the same evidence as photographs, so a person can judge what the numbers cannot settle.

It serves two readers of one record. An agent locates the change and names it. A person looks at the change and decides whether it is acceptable.

Neither reader wants the lab's opinion. The report presents evidence: the rendered pictures, the reference beside them, and the detail where the change lives.

## Two surfaces

The record is the product. The report is how a person reads it.

The record carries what was measured, and the report carries what was rendered. A reader takes whichever answers their question, and neither repeats the other.

## What it refuses to become

- A scoreboard that ranks renderers by one number.
- A dashboard whose chart is more prominent than the photograph.
- An editor that changes the picture it judges.
- A viewer that needs the renderer, the network, or the original inputs to open.

## The review page

The human surface is a light table, not a dashboard. A person opens it to look at photographs, and everything else on it is in the way.

| Stance | Why the page is shaped this way |
| --- | --- |
| The photograph is the content | Chrome that covers pixels takes the place of the evidence. |
| The surround stays neutral | A tint could be mistaken for a cast in the render itself. |
| Correspondence is never invented | A wipe between unrelated frames would claim a match that does not exist. |
| The page stands alone | A report MUST outlive the machine and the inputs that produced it. |

## Neighbors

| Surface | Owns | This report does not |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Comparison record | measurement, evidence, hashes | print its numbers |
| [Photo Report](photos/spec.md) | plain photo inventory and declared ROIs | measure or rank |
| GUI | making the render | edit, export, apply |
| Image editor | changing pixels | change anything |
